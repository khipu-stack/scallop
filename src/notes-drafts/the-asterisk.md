# The Asterisk: A Diﬀractive Prism

The asterisk * is a glyph of paradox—simultaneously a void and a plenitude, a silence and a scream. It is a symbol that gestures
beyond itself, inviting interpretation while resisting fixation. As a placeholder and a portal, it operates across linguistic, technological,
and cultural domains. This essay examines the asterisk’s evolution from an editorial tool to a marker of multiplicity, exploring its role in
language, algorithmic systems, and artistic practice. By analyzing the asterisk through linguistics, assemblage theory, diﬀraction,
Hauntology, and new materialism, it argues that this seemingly simple sign embodies the interdisciplinary ethos of media art: where
ambiguity is not a flaw but a foundation.
Linguistic Archaeology: The Asterisk as Omission and Amplification
Etymologically, the asterisk derives from the Greek asteriskos (‘little star’), rooted in the Proto-Indo-European ster-(star). Initially a
textual annotation, it was used by Aristarchus of Samothrace (2nd century BC), a philologist, to mark variant readings in Homer’s epics,
Origen adopted it in biblical analysis to indicate textual additions. Over time, its role expanded from marking anomalies and omissions
to signaling emphasis, multiplication, and censorship across diﬀerent linguistic systems.
The East Asian textual tradition followed a diﬀerent trajectory. In ancient Chinese civilization, the three-stroke thinking mode is
consistent with graphic expression. The origin of the three-stroke is unknown, and it is speculated that it was formed before the Xia
Dynasty. Related to it is the divination civilization, which is a representative summary of primitive civilization. The earliest was object
divination (such as yarrow), then symbol divination (such as three strokes), and finally text divination (such as I Ching). To know sixty-
four, you must know eight first, and to know eight, you must know three first, while Chinese character corrections relied on radicals and
punctuation rather than external symbols. This contrast reflects broader semiotic thinking: the former emphasizes the instrumentality
and abstract transplantation of symbols, while the latter focuses on the visual symbiosis of symbols and context. Curiously, in the digital
age, the asterisk can also be seen as composed of three strokes. This resonates with the ancient Chinese pattern.
In historical linguistics, the asterisk marks reconstructed proto-forms (*proto-Indo-European) and later, in generative grammar, denotes
ungrammatical constructions. Giorgio Graﬃ identifies its use in the works of pioneers such as Bloomfield and Benveniste, illustrating how its semantic expansion parallels shifts in linguistic paradigms.[^1]

Assemblage: The Asterisk as a Dynamic Signifier
The asterisk (*), when isolated, is simply a character with no inherent role. However, once embedded in a system, it becomes an active
part of an assemblage, shaping and being shaped by its context. According to Deleuze and Guattari’s A Thousand Plateaus, an
assemblage is a dynamic configuration where components interact to generate new meanings and functions. Isolated, the asterisk (*) is
merely a character; but within a system, it becomes an active part of an assemblage, shaping and being shaped by its context.
In written text, the asterisk denotes emphasis or omission. In mathematics, it represents multiplication. On social media, it serves
various roles, such as censoring words (f***). These examples illustrate how the asterisk’s meaning is not fixed but emergent, shaped by
its interactions within diﬀerent linguistic, technological, and cultural systems.

## Embracing Indeterminacy: Diﬀraction and Multiplicity in Structural Parallels

In digital world, the asterisk standing for any character or infinite possibility. A search for art may yield artifice, articulate, or artichoke—
an algorithmic serendipity that collapses rigid categorical boundaries. This function resonates with artistic methodologies, such as
Dadaist collage techniques, where found materials are fragmented and recombined to challenge norms. With Mallarmé reoriented
poetic combinatorics into a compositional process that led to the "constellations" of concrete poetry, reversing its philosophical
implications. Early Dadaism celebrated a colonially stereotyped language magic, but later it sought to formalize chance-driven poetics
and strip away mimetic elements. Hans Arp’s 1920 random paper scraps prefigure Tristan Tzara’s 1923 algorithmic approach in “Pour
faire un poème dadaïste,” where poetic elements are no longer fixed but freely permuted—transforming poetry into a dynamic
algorithm.
Tzara’s algorithm reimagines the Proteus verse by mathematically permuting texts at the word level. It applies the collage technique of
fragments its text and produces chaos instead of harmonious cosmic order. By redefining verse as an open process, Tzara transformed
his poem into a self-contained calculation—a poetic computer with random access memory conceived decades before Turing and von
Neumann. His instructions translate seamlessly from French into code, shifting from partial to full automation.[^2] 
William S. Burroughs recasts the chaos of Tzara’s poem as a shift from syntax to a pragmatic, performative operation. He describes the
aesthetic as a “mescaline hallucination”—“seeing colors, tasting sounds, smelling forms.” In doing so, cut-up poetics revives the
metaphysics of textual permutation, ultimately turning Tzara’s own approach against itself.
In physics, diﬀraction occurs when waves bend and create overlapping patterns upon encountering obstacles. Similarly, the asterisk
mirrors this behavior by acting as a symbolic aperture where meaning splinters into multiple trajectories. In language, it creates
ambiguity between primary and secondary meanings, while in computing it serves as a wildcard symbol, both systems embracing
unpredictable pathways and generating emergent outcomes from open-ended inputs.
The asterisk, therefore, is not just a tool but a philosophy, one that reveals linguistic, technological, and artistic systems. By embracing
the indeterminacy, artists resist fixed interpretations and instead engage in meaning-making as an ongoing, collaborative process.
The Asterisk in Algorithmic and AI Systems
The asterisk is a key tool in search algorithms because it serves as a wildcard that broadens query results. For example, a search for
‘climate * change’ might return results like ‘climate policy change’ or ‘climate and change’. However, from a New Materialist
perspective, search engines aren’t neutral. They are assemblages of users, algorithms, and databases that determine what information
is surfaced and what is hidden. This leads to the question: does an asterisk in algorithmic system give users more access, or does it
reinforce unseen structures of information control?
In AI-generated text, the asterisk plays a regulatory role in censorship. Language models might replace or mask certain words with an
asterisk to keep content within social norms. While this practice prevents oﬀensive content, it also raises ethical concerns: does the
asterisk in AI-driven censorship safeguard inclusivity, or does it encode ideological biases into digital speech? In both cases, the
asterisk actively shapes what information we see and how we communicate and governing the boundaries of discourse.
The Asterisk in Gender-Inclusive Language: A Symbolic Gesture?
Language has been granted too much powers.[^3] Linguistically, the asterisk is used in forms like *innen to promote gender inclusivity
and challenge the traditional masculine/feminine binary. Although it provides a way to represent non-binary identities, its eﬀectiveness is
debated: does *innen truly dismantle linguistic structures, or does it merely reconfigure them under a new norm? *innen is not an
isolated symbol, it’s part of a wider network of institutional policies, social movements, and digital platforms. While some government
bodies require its use and social media helps spread it, this practice might be more about elevating its symbolic rather than practical
significance. This leads to a critical question: is *innen a catalyst for genuine social change, or has it become a politically charged
emblem that reinforces symbolic inclusivity without enacting real transformation?


Hauntology and the Asterisk: A Symbol of the Unresolved
Beyond its immediate functions, the asterisk embodies Hauntological principles—Derrida’s concept of lingering pasts and unrealized
futures. It frequently marks absence, omission, or unrealized potential, as seen in redacted text, missing historical records, and linguistic
placeholders. This spectral quality raises profound questions: does the asterisk signify a “lost future,” a reality that could have been but
never materialized?
By continuously shifting across disciplines and media, * reinforces a state of ambiguity, contributing to a world where information is
increasingly fragmented and “de-materialized.” In digital spaces, where omitted characters or censored words are veiled by *, the
symbol becomes both a mask and a trace, concealing meaning while hinting at its presence. This paradox situates the asterisk at the
heart of contemporary information politics—does it serve as a tool for self-determination, or does it enforce new regimes of digital
control?
Conclusion: Cosmic Metaphor and Unfinished Universe
The asterisk exemplifies how a seemingly simple symbol operates across linguistic, technological, and cultural domains, existing not as
an isolated sign but as an interactive, adaptable, and evolving entity. As a wildcard, it expands possibility and defies categorical rigidity.
As a diﬀractive mark, it generates interference and noise between primary and secondary meanings. As a regulatory tool, it participates
in algorithmic censorship and linguistic governance. As a Hauntological trace, it reminds us of that which is absent yet still exerts
influence.
Ultimately, the asterisk occupies a liminal space between empowerment and restriction, visibility and erasure, expansion and
containment. Whether functioning as a censoring device, a mathematical operator, or a structural modifier in digital text, the asterisk
continues to shape—and be shaped by—the systems in which it operates. It is a site of contestation, simultaneously opening
possibilities while enforcing constraints, embodying the paradox of language, technology, and power. Beyond linguistics and
technology, the asterisk operates cosmologically.
“CONSTELLATION” describes both the spatial arrangement of the page elements and literally the arrangement of the stars in the
firmament. This metaphor aligns with the concept of *the open work*, theorized by Umberto Eco, where art remains deliberately
unfinished, demanding audience participation. In media art, such openness is vital; interactive installations, generative algorithms, and
participatory films thrive on the asterisk’s ethos, transforming spectators into collaborators.
The cross-civilization journey of this "little star" ultimately reveals the dual paradox of symbolic effectiveness: it is both a dialectical
carrier of concealment/revelation and a field of cultural game of form/function.
